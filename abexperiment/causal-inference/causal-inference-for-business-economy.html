Causal Inference in Healthcare [cite: 327]

Exploring the frontiers of personalized medicine: Target Trial Emulation, Digital Twins, Epidemiology, ITE, and Wearables.

The State of Causal Inference in Medicine

Across all five topics, the strongest recurring finding is that causal validity is limited less by model capacity than by study design, measurement quality, time-zero alignment, treatment definition, and domain knowledge [cite: 330].

Target Trial Emulation (TTE)

For electronic health records, target trial emulation has become the dominant organizing framework for causal effectiveness questions because it forces investigators to define eligibility, treatment strategies, assignment, follow-up, outcomes, causal contrast, and analysis before looking at data [cite: 331].

  • In rare diseases, it is particularly valuable because randomization is often infeasible, slow, or ethically difficult, but observational data are also intrinsically fragile [cite: 368].
  • The rare-disease version of TTE therefore succeeds less often through "big data" in the usual sense than through small-but-high-quality longitudinal cohorts with explicit protocol emulation and linkage [cite: 369].
  • Recent methodological work also matters because it clarifies that EHR-based TTE is not guaranteed merely by using the label [cite: 382].

Precision Oncology Digital Twins

For precision oncology, digital twins are moving from static predictive surrogates toward dynamic, multiscale patient representations that integrate imaging, molecular data, therapy response, and sometimes mechanistic tumor biology [cite: 337].

  • A clinically meaningful oncology twin cannot be only a high-performing predictive model [cite: 416].
  • It must respond correctly to interventions [cite: 417].
  • Recent oncology reviews define the opportunity clearly: twins that are dynamically updated, intervention-aware, verifiable, and uncertainty-quantified could support individualized treatment selection and virtual trial simulation [cite: 339].

Causal AI in Epidemiology

In epidemiology, a productive synthesis is emerging between traditional causal reasoning and automated discovery [cite: 341].

  • Bradford Hill’s viewpoints remain influential as an interpretive framework, but modern epidemiology continues to stress that they are not a mechanical checklist [cite: 342].
  • The highest-confidence near-term role for automated discovery is not "fully automatic causal truth," but hypothesis generation, structure refinement, robustness checking, and expert-augmented causal model construction [cite: 344].
  • The final product should be a causal model that is interpretable, intervention-relevant, and transparent about assumptions [cite: 488].

Predicting Individualized Treatment Effects (ITE)

For individualized treatment effects, the field is mature enough to move beyond average treatment effects, but not mature enough to treat individual effect predictions as routine clinical truth [cite: 346].

  • Evaluation remains difficult because counterfactuals are unobserved [cite: 348].
  • The emerging evaluation literature stresses that outcome prediction quality is not the same as treatment-effect prediction quality [cite: 528].
  • In practice, the most robust clinical pathway is often triangulation: derive heterogeneity patterns in an RCT if possible, transport or recalibrate them in observational data, and externally validate in another cohort or trial-like dataset [cite: 531].

Wearables and Causal Triggers

Wearables make causal research attractive because they provide dense, temporally ordered, real-world measurements of physiology and behavior [cite: 550].

  • The strongest recent disease-specific evidence comes from inflammatory bowel disease and rheumatoid arthritis, where prospective studies found that heart rate, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, activity, oxygenation, and related physiological signals changed before flare onset [cite: 551].
  • Many wearables currently detect preclinical state change rather than an intervention target [cite: 557].
  • A causal trigger is something whose modification would change flare risk [cite: 556].
