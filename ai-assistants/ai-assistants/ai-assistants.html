Five types of LLM applications

Read slide text
AI Assistants 0 1 · T Y P E S Five types of LLM applications Search Find information using an LLM and a vector database. Answer Provide factual responses, grounded in your knowledge. Recommend Take the user's preferences into account and generate personalized responses. Plan Let users define goals and build a plan that weighs several factors and combines tasks. Complete task Not only build the plan but carry out the tasks on the user's behalf. Increasing complexity → Each type builds on the one before it. An assistant that plans must also search and answer well. 3

Beyond keyword search

Read slide text
AI Assistants 0 1 · T Y P E S Beyond keyword search User asks: “Can I take my dog on a flight to Paris next month, and what do I need?” Keyword search • Matches the words “dog”, “flight”, “Paris” • Returns a list of links to read • Can't tell which rules apply to you • Stops at the results page AI assistant • Understands the intent: travelling with a pet • Answers directly, citing the airline's pet policy • Asks the dog's size to check cabin or hold rules • Offers to add the pet to the booking 5

From answering to acting: levels of autonomy

Read slide text
AI Assistants 0 1 · T Y P E S From answering to acting: levels of autonomy 1 Inform Answers and explains; the user does everything “Here is our refund policy.” 2 Suggest Recommends what to do next “You're eligible: request a refund here.” 3 Draft for approval Prepares the action; the user confirms “I've filled in the refund. Submit?” 4 Act, then report Acts within set limits and reports back “Refund of $40 issued to your card.” 5 Act autonomously Runs multi-step tasks on a schedule “I processed 120 refunds overnight.” Match the level to the risk: money, personal data and external messages usually need approval, at least until the assistant has earned trust. 6

Slide 9 of 18

Conversation design patterns

Read slide text
AI Assistants 0 2 · F E AT U R E S Conversation design patterns Multi-step flows Guide the user through a process one step at a time, keeping track of where they are. Conditional branching Take different paths based on answers, eligibility or account data. Clarifying questions Ask one short question when the request is ambiguous, rather than guessing. Progress updates Tell users what is happening on long tasks, and how long it will take. Consistent tone Professional, on-brand language; the same voice in every channel and language. Graceful fallback When unsure, say so, offer options, or hand off to a person. 9

Memory and personalization

Read slide text
AI Assistants 0 2 · F E AT U R E S Memory and personalization Session memory What was said in this conversation Result: Follow-up questions work without repeating details Long-term memory Preferences and history across conversations Result: “Book my usual aisle seat.” User profile Account data from your systems (CRM, orders) Result: Answers reflect the user's plan, region and status Give users control: say what is remembered, let users view and delete it, ask consent before storing sensitive details, and never use memory to surprise or pressure people. 10

Slide 11 of 18

Escalating to a live agent

Read slide text
AI Assistants 0 2 · F E AT U R E S Escalating to a live agent When to hand off Low confidence: The assistant can't find a grounded answer The user asks: Always honour a request for a person Sensitive topics: Complaints, legal threats, distress, safety Repeated failure: Two or three turns without progress High value: Large orders, VIP customers, cancellations A good handoff • Pass a short summary, the user's details and what was already tried • The user never repeats themselves • Tell the user what happens next and how long it takes • Outside staffed hours, offer a callback or ticket • Review handoffs weekly: each one is a lesson for the assistant 11

AI Assistants 0 3 · E X A M P L E S Worked example: an agent that updates a website daily 1 Trigger Runs every morning on a schedule 2 Gather Pulls new products, prices and news from source systems 3 Draft Writes page updates and descriptions in the brand voice 4 Check Verifies facts, links, prices and brand rules 5 Approve A person approves changes above a set threshold 6 Publish and report Publishes and sends a summary of what changed This is the “Execute” type: it uses search, answers, planning and integrations together, with a human approval step for larger changes. 14

Four kinds of metrics

Read slide text
AI Assistants 0 4 · M E T R I C S Four kinds of metrics Quality • Answer accuracy • Groundedness (answers backed by sources) • Task success rate User experience • Customer satisfaction (CSAT) • Thumbs-up rate • Repeat usage Business impact • Containment (resolved without a person) • Time to resolution • Conversion or revenue influenced Operations • Response time • Cost per conversation • Escalation and safety incidents Track at least one metric from each group. A fast, cheap assistant that gives wrong answers, or an accurate one nobody uses, is not a success. 15

The right metric for each type

Read slide text
AI Assistants 0 4 · M E T R I C S The right metric for each type Type The key question Primary metrics Search Did the right result appear near the top? Retrieval hit rate, click-through Answer Was the answer correct and supported? Accuracy, groundedness, citation accuracy Recommend Did users act on the suggestions? Acceptance rate, conversion, diversity Plan Was the plan usable as given? Plan acceptance, number of user edits Execute Did the task finish correctly? Task success rate, errors, rollbacks, approvals needed As types get more complex, measure outcomes (was the task done right?) rather than single responses. 16

Key takeaways

Read slide text
Key takeaways 1 AI assistants go beyond keywords: they search, answer, recommend, plan and act. 2 Each step up in type needs more data, reasoning, integration and safeguards. 3 Good assistants guide, gather, personalize, solve and hand off gracefully. 4 Start where questions repeat and knowledge is documented, then extend to actions. 5 Measure quality, user experience, business impact and cost together. 18
