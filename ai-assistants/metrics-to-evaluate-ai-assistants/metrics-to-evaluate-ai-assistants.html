Metrics to Evaluate AI Assistants 0 1 · C O N V E R S AT I O N Read the metrics together, not alone Signal Could mean Check it with High self-service rate Users may be giving up, not getting helped Pair with task completion and satisfaction Short conversations Fast answers, or users leaving early Pair with completion: short and complete is the goal High engagement Users love it, or keep returning because it failed Pair with repeat-contact rate on the same issue High satisfaction Only happy users may leave ratings Check the response rate and read unrated conversations Every metric can be gamed or misread. Slide 8 of 18

Response time and cost

Read slide text
Metrics to Evaluate AI Assistants 0 2 · R E S P O N S E Response time and cost Time to first word How long before text starts to appear. Drives how fast the assistant feels. Total response time Until the full answer is ready, including retrieval and tool calls. Tokens per response Input (prompt + context + history) and output tokens. Cost per resolved task Total cost of all turns ÷ tasks completed: the number that matters to the business. Report percentiles, not just averages. A median (p50) of 2 seconds can hide a p95 of 12 seconds: one user in twenty waits six times longer. Track p50 and p95 for time, and cost per task alongside cost per response. 8

Technical metrics to evaluate LLMs

Read slide text
Metrics to Evaluate AI Assistants 0 3 · M O D E L Technical metrics to evaluate LLMs # Metric What it measures Used for Better 1 BLEU How many word sequences (n-grams) in the output also appear in a reference text. Precision-based. Translation Higher 2 GLEU Google-BLEU: a sentence-level version of BLEU that uses both precision and recall of n-grams. Translation, grammar correction Higher 3 ROUGE Overlap of n-grams or longest common sequences with the reference. Recall-based. Summarization Higher 4 METEOR Matches words exactly, by stem and by synonym, then combines precision and recall. Translation, closer to human judgement Higher 5 Perplexity How well the model predicts the next word of real text; how “surprised” it is. Comparing language models Lower Decoding the mystery of LLMs: these metrics came from translation and summarization research and need a reference text. 9

Why word overlap isn't enough

Read slide text
Metrics to Evaluate AI Assistants 0 3 · M O D E L Why word overlap isn't enough SAME MEANING, LITTLE OVERLAP Reference: “The meeting has been moved to Friday.” Assistant: “We've rescheduled it for Friday.” Overlap scores rate this answer poorly, though a person would call it correct. The reverse also happens: high overlap with one wrong number. Still useful for: translation, short fixed-form outputs, and tracking regressions on a stable test set. Perplexity is useful when training or comparing models. Modern methods Semantic similarity Compare meaning with embeddings (e.g. BERTScore) LLM as judge A strong model grades against a rubric Groundedness Is every claim supported by the sources? Task benchmarks Domain question sets with known answers 10

Slide 14 of 18

Ranking metrics for recommendations

Read slide text
Metrics to Evaluate AI Assistants 0 5 · R E C O M M E N DAT I O N Ranking metrics for recommendations Focus on the quality of recommendations DCG / NDCG Discounted cumulative gain Considers both the relevance and the position of recommendations. A higher DCG means the most relevant items are ranked higher. NDCG scales it from 0 to 1. Use when: Items have graded relevance MRR Mean reciprocal rank The average reciprocal rank of the first relevant item in the list. A higher MRR means the relevant item is found sooner. Use when: Users want one right item MAP@K Mean average precision at K Precision (relevance) averaged across all positions up to K in the list, then across users. A higher MAP@K is better. Use when: Users browse several items 14

Running an evaluation programme

Read slide text
Metrics to Evaluate AI Assistants 0 6 · I N P R A C T I C E Running an evaluation programme Offline: before release • Golden set of real questions, including ones with no answer • Run on every change to model, prompt, index or tools • Block release if key scores drop Online: in production • Track conversation, response and cost metrics live • A/B test changes on a share of users • Sample conversations for weekly human review Close the loop: every failure found online becomes a new test case offline, so the same mistake is caught before the next release. 16

Key takeaways

Read slide text
Key takeaways 1 Measure at four levels: conversation, response, model and components. 2 Task completion and satisfaction tell you if the assistant works; read them together. 3 Score each response for accuracy and completeness, with time and cost alongside. 4 BLEU and ROUGE need references; use LLM judges and groundedness for open answers. 5 Pick one main metric, guard it with others, and close the loop from production to tests. 18
