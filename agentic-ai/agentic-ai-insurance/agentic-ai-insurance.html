Six use cases and autonomy levels

The deck distinguishes copilots that suggest, supervised agents with approval checkpoints, and autonomous agents acting within defined limits. These labels describe the proposed design, not a universal approval for a use case.

  • Claims intake and triage
  • Simple claims settlement
  • Fraud screening
  • Quote and policy assistant
  • Underwriting copilot
  • Policy document drafting

SLIDE 4 / 7

Inside the simple-claims settlement agent

The flagship workflow connects evidence gathering to a defined action, with a person at a critical checkpoint. The sequence below summarizes the source slide.

  1. Check the policy and cover for the reported loss
  2. Verify the event using suitable external data
  3. Screen for duplicate claims and fraud signals
  4. Calculate a proposed payout and draft the settlement
  5. Have a claims professional approve payment

SLIDE 7 / 7

A practical roadmap

Start with claims intake and triage plus underwriting summaries. Reviewed simple-claims settlement and fraud screening, then broader claim types and quote assistance. The deck’s 90-day, 3–12-month and longer-term stages are planning suggestions, not delivery guarantees.

Questions answered

Where should insurance teams start?

Claims intake and triage plus underwriting summaries. Use a bounded pilot with a baseline and a named owner.

What is the featured workflow?

Simple-claims settlement agent. Have a claims professional approve payment.

How is value measured?

Claim cycle time, Straight-through share, Leakage identified in audits, Customer satisfaction. Compare benefits with error rates, review effort and operating cost.

What does autonomous mean in this deck?

The agent acts within set limits while humans monitor. It does not mean unlimited authority or an absence of oversight.
