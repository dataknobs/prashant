Six use cases and autonomy levels

The deck distinguishes copilots that suggest, supervised agents with approval checkpoints, and autonomous agents acting within defined limits. These labels describe the proposed design, not a universal approval for a use case.

Inside the shopping assistant

The flagship workflow connects evidence gathering to a defined action, with a person at a critical checkpoint. The sequence below summarizes the source slide.

  1. Clarify the occasion, budget and requirements
  2. Search the catalog and check nearby stock
  3. Build a proposed basket with alternatives
  4. Ask the shopper to review and confirm
  5. Arrange the chosen delivery or pickup

SLIDE 7 / 7

A practical roadmap

Start with store-associate assistance and product discovery. Approved replenishment and personalized marketing, then constrained pricing and fulfillment orchestration. The deck’s 90-day, 3–12-month and longer-term stages are planning suggestions, not delivery guarantees.

Questions answered

Where should retail and e-commerce teams start?

Store-associate assistance and product discovery. Use a bounded pilot with a baseline and a named owner.

What is the featured workflow?

Shopping assistant. Arrange the chosen delivery or pickup.

How is value measured?

Conversion rate, Average basket value, Stock availability, Cost to serve. Compare benefits with error rates, review effort and operating cost.

What does autonomous mean in this deck?

The agent acts within set limits while humans monitor. It does not mean unlimited authority or an absence of oversight.
