Inside the storm-response agent

The flagship workflow connects evidence gathering to a defined action, with a person at a critical checkpoint. The sequence below summarizes the source slide.

  1. Combine weather forecasts with asset-condition data
  2. Estimate likely line failures
  3. Propose crew and equipment positions
  4. Have the control-room supervisor approve the plan
  5. Communicate verified restoration estimates

SLIDE 7 / 7

A practical roadmap

Start with outage and billing q&a plus maintenance work-order drafting. Reviewed storm-response planning and meter analytics before broader scheduling and grid-operation assistance. The deck’s 90-day, 3–12-month and longer-term stages are planning suggestions, not delivery guarantees.

Questions answered

Where should energy and utilities teams start?

Outage and billing Q&A plus maintenance work-order drafting. Use a bounded pilot with a baseline and a named owner.

What is the featured workflow?

Storm-response agent. Communicate verified restoration estimates.

How is value measured?

Outage duration and frequency, Restoration time, Renewable curtailment, Maintenance backlog by risk. Compare benefits with error rates, review effort and operating cost.

What does autonomous mean in this deck?

The agent acts within set limits while humans monitor. It does not mean unlimited authority or an absence of oversight.
