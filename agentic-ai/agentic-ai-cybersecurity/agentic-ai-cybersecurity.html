Six use cases and autonomy levels

The deck distinguishes copilots that suggest, supervised agents with approval checkpoints, and autonomous agents acting within defined limits. These labels describe the proposed design, not a universal approval for a use case.

  • Phishing triage
  • Alert investigation
  • Incident response
  • Vulnerability management
  • Identity and access review
  • Threat intelligence

Inside the phishing triage agent

The flagship workflow connects evidence gathering to a defined action, with a person at a critical checkpoint. The sequence below summarizes the source slide.

  1. Analyze the reported email, links and attachments
  2. Check sender reputation and threat intelligence
  3. Classify the report and explain the verdict
  4. Escalate uncertain or malicious cases to an analyst
  5. Apply authorized response actions and notify the user

Risks and guardrails

Treat email and web content as untrusted, give agents separate identities with limited privileges, constrain disruptive responses and measure missed attacks. SLIDE 7 / 7

A practical roadmap

Start with phishing triage and threat-intelligence summaries. Investigation drafting and access reviews before limited playbook response and proposed vulnerability fixes. The deck’s 90-day, 3–12-month and longer-term stages are planning suggestions, not delivery guarantees.

Questions answered

Where should cybersecurity teams start?

Phishing triage and threat-intelligence summaries. Use a bounded pilot with a baseline and a named owner.

What is the featured workflow?

Phishing triage agent. Apply authorized response actions and notify the user.

How is value measured?

Mean time to detect, Mean time to respond, Alerts per analyst, Verdict accuracy. Compare benefits with error rates, review effort and operating cost.

What does autonomous mean in this deck?

The agent acts within set limits while humans monitor. It does not mean unlimited authority or an absence of oversight.
