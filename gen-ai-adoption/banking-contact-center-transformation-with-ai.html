How banks are defining AI’s role in the contact center

Across Banking and Financial Services, organizations are converging on a pragmatic definition of AI in the contact center over the next 2–3 years: AI is being funded first as an efficiency and risk-control engine, and then expanded into an experience and revenue engine once the “context + controls + continuity” foundations are in place. This sequencing shows up in both industry surveys and early deployment case studies.

A consistent theme is that cost takeout (or cost avoidance) is the first explicit objective, largely because contact centers are high-volume, labor-intensive, and operationally measurable.

The Efficiency-First Mandate

  • Gartner Forecast (by 2029): "Agentic AI" is expected to autonomously resolve 80% of common service issues, driving a 30% operational cost reduction.
  • EY-Parthenon (2025): 56% of identified banking GenAI use cases target cost/time efficiency, and 54% are internal-specific.

At the same time, the target state is evolving toward AI-enabled CX uplift plus relationship growth. The end-state goal: turn the contact center into an omnichannel value hub that balances human and digital interactions.

Continuity Across Voice, Chat, App, and Branch

Banks are pursuing continuity across service channels because the biggest CX failures—and cost drivers—occur at handoffs. Customers repeat themselves, re-authenticate, get inconsistent answers, or are misrouted.

Leading programs are organizing around a single "customer context spine": identity + authentication state, recent activity, intent, product holdings, journey stage, and interaction history. Banks are also modernizing the "front door" via voice entry, intent capture, and routing (e.g., AI-IVR that replaces push-button navigation with natural speech intent routing and multi-turn follow-ups).

Balancing Speed, Empathy, and Resolution Quality

The industry’s operational learning is that speed alone is an unstable optimization target. Faster handling without empathy and quality creates repeat contacts, escalations, complaints, and mistrust—especially during fraud, disputes, or financial hardship.

Sentiment over Handle Time

Shifting from AHT to balancing resolution, sentiment, and outcomes. Sentiment is becoming the primary metric.

Empathy Instrumentation

Scoring agent empathy from -100 to +100 to balance AI-driven efficiency with deep human engagement.

Risk Segmentation

High-risk intents (account takeover) trigger AI-to-human escalation with full context transfer.

Where Continuity Still Breaks

  • Self-service failure: Over 60% of customers rate chatbot experiences as "average," and 61% contact agents because chatbots failed to resolve their issue.
  • Operational fragmentation: Over 80% of a representative's workday is consumed by repetitive manual tasks, leaving less than 10% for up-selling and cross-selling.
  • Authentication bottlenecks: Channel jumping often forces re-authentication. Passive voice biometrics are emerging as a key solution to buy back time and reduce security-service complaints.

Where AI is Delivering the Most Efficiency Impact

Within BFS contact centers, the highest-impact efficiency gains are clustering in four zones:

  1. Agent & Supervisor Augmentation: Keeping humans in control while reducing search burden. (e.g., 5-minute reduction in handle time per interaction, 2 hours/day saved for supervisors).
  2. Conversation Analytics at Scale: Shifting QA from sampling to near 100% coverage, turning unstructured conversations into operational signals.
  3. Identity & Intent Routing: Using natural-language and multi-turn clarification to reduce misroutes and friction.
  4. Automated Post-Interaction Work: Summarization and categorization.

Scaling Constraints and The Remediation Playbook

Banks are not primarily blocked by "model capability" in 2026; they are blocked by structure.

Structural Bottlenecks

  • Data readiness and access constraints: Regulatory, privacy, and high-quality data access remain top barriers.
  • High "pilot-to-production attrition": Only 16% of identified use cases make it to implementation, and 40% of those fail to reach desired results.
  • Knowledge management quality: GenAI requires a foundation of relevant, well-organized knowledge, which is often trapped in chat logs or rigid scripts.
  • Risk, compliance, and governance: Navigating NIST frameworks and EU AI Act transparency expectations limits the pace of adoption.

The Remediation Playbook

Banks successfully scaling AI are turning "AI features" into "AI-enabled operating systems" by:

  1. Consolidating and standardizing the knowledge substrate (structuring for RAG).
  2. Building "workflow-grade" integration (retrieval + orchestration, not just chat).
  3. Formalizing governance and executive ownership (75% of banks now have formal GenAI committees).
  4. Shifting from "static validation" to "continuous monitoring + fallback design."
  5. Designing measurement systems that reward resolution quality, not just speed.

Turning Service into Retention and Revenue

Banks consistently describe service interactions as "moments of truth." AI is allowing banks to operationalize this at scale through a real-time "next-best-action" layer that uses customer profiles, interaction context, and business rules to recommend actions.

The clearest link between service and revenue is: Service Quality → Loyalty/Retention Proxies → Revenue.

Banks are looking for revenue proof in two clean places:

  • Retention protection in high-risk moments: Fraud, disputes, and hardship where empathy drives trust and reduces attrition.
  • Share-of-wallet recovery: Context-aware orchestration ensuring the right channel and offer treatment.

The Evolution of Contact Center Roles

As AI takes on "first draft" work, the contact center org structure is shifting from a labor-centric model to a product + governance + continuous-improvement model.

Supervisors

Moving from queue control to coaching and operations leadership, aided by AI copilots that surface real-time insights.

Quality Assurance (QA)

Shifting from sampled scoring to 100% evidence-based monitoring. QA becomes highly explainable and coaching-oriented.

Workforce Management (WFM)

Evolving toward scenario planning and managing a blended workforce of human and automated agents.

Product & Platform

Running the AI contact center as a product. New roles are emerging.

Emerging Bank Contact Center Roles

  • AI Product Owner for Service: Owns the AI roadmap, value measurement, and cross-channel rollout.
  • Conversation Designer: Orchestrates voice/chat flows and channel transitions.
  • Knowledge Engineer: Curates and structures RAG data and policy content.
  • Intelligent QA Lead: Owns automated evaluation logic and bias checks.
  • Model-Risk Liaison: Bridges service ops and model risk management.
  • Prompt & Governance Lead: Maintains guardrails and ensures regulatory compliance.
