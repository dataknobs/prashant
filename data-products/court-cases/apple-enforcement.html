04

What the cases share

THEME 01

Implementation, not ownership

Regulators seldom claim that Apple is prohibited from operating a curated store. Instead, they point to issues with its implementation, such as anti-steering suppression, asymmetric ATT consent, and overly broad restraints. France and Italy deemed privacy a valid concern, but found ATT's enforcement to be excessive, while the Dutch case focused on unfair conditions rather than the App Store as a whole.

THEME 02

Anti-steering is the battleground

The theory most frequently cited is that Apple prohibits developers from informing users about less expensive options outside of the app. This theory is central to the EU music-streaming fine, the DMA decision, the Dutch and Russian legal cases, and Epic's notable victory in the US, which was later upheld through a contempt ruling after Apple's supposed compliance effectively nullified the court order.

THEME 03

Security & privacy as defense

Apple's consistent defenses of platform integrity, user privacy, and developer tool fees effectively countered Epic's federal monopoly claims, but faltered when accused of restricting competition or favoring themselves unfairly.

THEME 04

Remedies over fines

The most impactful results don't always come with the largest financial rewards: anti-steering orders, Dutch payment opening, Epic contempt rulings, Masimo import exclusion, Brazil's no-charger sales suspension, and NLRB settlement reshaping internal worker policies.

THEME 05

Consumer cases bite the product

Batterygate, water-resistance claims, 'Error 53' repair rights, and Brazil's charger-free policy are all examples of how hardware sales and descriptions are influenced by small yet impactful factors, affecting packaging, marketing, and warranty practices.

THEME 06

Europe is the center of gravity

The largest reviewed results are in Europe, where the playbook progresses from abusive restraints to ATT implementation, DMA gatekeeper responsibilities, and state-aid tax recovery. In the US, the track record on monopoly theory varies, with significant impacts seen in injunctions, settlements, ITC remedies, and labor issues.
