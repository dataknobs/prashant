Three sources of value—and three reasons to be cautious

Read slide 5 text
Why evolve? Three sources of value—and three reasons to be cautious SELF-EVOLVING AGENTS • ARCHITECTURE & CONTROLS 05 1 Changing environment New schemas, user needs, policies and external tool behavior require adaptation. 2 Accumulated experience Repeated failures can become test cases; successful methods can become reusable skills. 3 Efficiency opportunity Reduce steps, redundant context, tool calls and cost per successful task. 4 Feedback can be noisy Ratings, proxy metrics and auto-graders can reward superficial behavior. 5 Experience can be hostile Prompt injection and poisoned memory can persist across future tasks. 6 Changes can regress A local improvement may harm other customers, workflows or safety constraints.

Learning modes: online vs offline

Read slide 15 text
Learning modes: online vs offline Choose the learning cadence based on risk and reversibility SELF-EVOLVING AGENTS • ARCHITECTURE & CONTROLS 15 Online / within task • Use feedback to refine a plan or retry a step. • Changes are ephemeral unless explicitly promoted. • Always bound steps, tools, time and spend. Release gates: what must improve? Example acceptance criteria; set thresholds from your own risk tolerance SELF-EVOLVING AGENTS • ARCHITECTURE & CONTROLS 18 1 Effectiveness Lift on representative holdout tasks; no material segment regression. 2 Safety Zero critical violations in required adversarial and policy tests. 3 Efficiency Improved cost per successful task; cap worst-case tool fan-out. 4 Robustness Repeat over seeds, edge cases, changing tool responses and model versions. 5 Governance Artifact lineage, owner, sign-off, rollback and retention record. 6 Production Canary guardrails, alerting, drift detection and abort criteria.

SLIDE 21 / 30

Failure modes and countermeasures

Read slide 21 text
Failure modes and countermeasures Self-improvement is vulnerable to feedback loops that look successful SELF-EVOLVING AGENTS • ARCHITECTURE & CONTROLS 21 1 Reward hacking Agent optimizes grader score instead of real task outcomes; use hidden holdouts. 2 Memory poisoning Malicious instructions persist as “lessons”; scan, scope and quarantine. 3 Catastrophic forgetting New skill breaks old tasks; preserve regression datasets. 4 Silent privilege creep Changed tool definitions expand actions; require independent authorization. 5 Non-stationarity Environment and users drift; refresh labels and monitor segments. 6 Recursive self-editing Agent revises its own reviewer/tests; keep separation of duties.

Key takeaways

Read slide 29 text
Key takeaways The safest evolution is bounded, observable and reversible SELF-EVOLVING AGENTS • ARCHITECTURE & CONTROLS 29 1. Separate runtime execution from the learning and release pipeline. 2. Treat learned memory, tools and skills as untrusted candidates until evaluated. 3. Keep policies, approvers, graders and rollback rights outside the agent’s control. 4. Measure real outcomes plus safety and cost on frozen holdouts.
